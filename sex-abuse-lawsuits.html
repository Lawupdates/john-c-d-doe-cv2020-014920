How to find public records in a sexual abuse lawsuit

  1. Find the court and case number. Start with the caption on a court document or the case number in a reliable report. Captions such as Jane Doe and John Doe can appear in many unrelated cases.
  2. Find the docket in the right court system. Use the county or state judiciary for a state lawsuit. Use PACER for a federal district, bankruptcy, or appellate case. A related bankruptcy or appeal can have a different case number.
  3. Open the filed document. Record the title, filing date, PDF page, and docket entry number where available. Some records require access through the clerk, and some are restricted or sealed.
  • Federal cases. Use the U.S. Courts guide to PACER or search the PACER Case Locator for federal dockets or browse selected federal opinions at GovInfo.
  • State cases. Use the court directory for all 50 states and D.C. to reach the local judiciary.
  • Examples of official state portals. Arizona Public Access, California court records lookup guide, Maryland Judiciary records, and New York eCourts.

What the court record can tell you

What did a party allege or dispute?
Read the complaint and the answer together. A complaint sets out the plaintiff’s allegations and requested relief. An answer states the defendant’s responses and may raise defenses.
What did the judge or jury decide?
Read the order, verdict form, or judgment for the particular issue and outcome. For appeals, read the appellate opinion and check the trial docket for what happened after remand, if any.
Was there a settlement?
A settlement notice reports a development in the case. Check for a filed agreement, approval order, stipulation, or dismissal entry as applicable. Some terms may be unavailable in the public record.
Did a lookback law apply?
A revival provision can reopen certain time-barred civil claims. The Maryland, California, and New York decisions above illustrate why the exact statute, jurisdiction, and procedural history matter. A court’s ruling on timeliness answers a different question from liability.
