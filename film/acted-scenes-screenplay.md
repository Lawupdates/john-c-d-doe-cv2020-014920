# THE CASE THEY CAN'T SILENCE
## Feature screenplay — acted scenes and character bible
**Development draft · Target runtime: 90 minutes · All dialogue below is invented for dramatization, not a transcript.**

**Important:** This film is a fictionalized dramatization inspired by contested allegations and public case proceedings, *not* a documentary. The pending litigation has no final adjudication of disputed conduct. No actor is authorized to imitate a real person's voice or suggest real footage.

### Film grammar
Show actors interacting continuously inside locations. Use shot/reverse-shot, reactions, overlapping natural dialogue, moving camera, physical actions, motivated close-ups and ambient sound. No slideshow, stock footage montage as the main storytelling format, robotic narration, talking head, or lengthy on-screen legal text. Real court documents are separate source inserts. 1983 footage warm 35mm grain; present-day cool naturalistic photography. Original music only.

### Character bible — performing roles
- **Young Daniel**: a fictional nine-year-old representative of the claimant as a child, curious, cautious, likes baseball, wants to belong. No graphic abuse scenes.
- **Daniel (adult)**: a fictionalized survivor character, 60s, wry, fiercely independent, detail-oriented, sometimes overwhelmed but purposeful. Low, dry, original actor voice.
- **Evelyn (mother)**: thoughtful, protective, working-class warmth; she believes the agency's promises.
- **Ms. Bell (agency coordinator)**: outwardly professional, efficient, overburdened. Not presented as knowingly facilitating wrongdoing.
- **The assigned mentor**: played by an actor; not a photographic or vocal copy of Italo Arbulu. Keep allegations clearly attributed, never create invented admissions or recreate unproven sexual misconduct.
- **The Civil Attorney**: fictional composite; persuasive courtroom manner, careful speech, competing views over settlement.
- **The Bankruptcy Attorney**: fictional composite; analytical, terse, strong procedural knowledge.
- **The Trustee**: fictionalized representative of court-appointed trustee function. Professional, pragmatic, and not villain-coded; show the differing incentives without invented dishonesty.
- **The Mediator**: fictionalized neutral. Never put unverifiable private statements in the mouth of a named real mediator.
- **Defense Counsel**: fictional composite; polished, tough, plausible good-faith litigation positions.
Named real people and organizations may be identified in sourced title cards or document references, not as the speakers of invented private discussions.

## ACT I — HOPE AND SECRETS

### SCENE 1 — INT. PHOENIX MENTORING OFFICE — AFTERNOON — JANUARY 1983
Sunlight slides across a beige carpet. A paper fan clicks above an aging desk. Nine-year-old DANIEL sits in a too-large chair, rocking his feet. His mother EVELYN has a folded application on her lap.

MS. BELL enters, smiling but already turning a page in a file.

**BELL:** You must be Daniel.
**DANIEL:** Depends who wants to know.
**EVELYN:** Daniel.
**BELL:** (smiles) Fair enough. I'm the person who helps find you somebody to spend time with.
**DANIEL:** What if we don't like the same stuff?
**BELL:** Then you can show each other something new.
**EVELYN:** And somebody checks these people?
**BELL:** We have a process, Mrs. — (she checks the file) — yes. We have a process.

Daniel traces the seam in the chair with his finger.

**DANIEL:** Does he like baseball?
**BELL:** You can ask him yourself.

Bell closes the file. HOLD on the closed file — no sinister musical sting. CUT TO BLACK.

TITLE CARD: 'Inspired by the plaintiff's account of a mentoring match dated January 28, 1983. Dialogue and personnel are dramatized.'

### SCENE 2 — INT. SMALL ARIZONA HOUSE — NIGHT — OCTOBER 2026
A screen illuminates ADULT DANIEL's face. A legal email is open. He moves his reading glasses closer. A half-finished mug sits beside folders.

The phone rings. He lets it ring once before answering.

**DANIEL:** Tell me they didn't file it.
**BANKRUPTCY ATTORNEY (PHONE):** I haven't seen an order approving it.
**DANIEL:** That's not what I asked.
**BANKRUPTCY ATTORNEY:** I know.
**DANIEL:** They call it an agreement. They haven't shown me the agreement.
**BANKRUPTCY ATTORNEY:** There may be a difference between what the trustee can settle and what you can be made to sign.
**DANIEL:** You make it sound simple.
**BANKRUPTCY ATTORNEY:** It isn't simple. That's why we need the actual document.

Daniel opens a file labeled JULY 8 and stares at it.

**DANIEL:** I was nine when this started.

## ACT II — THE RECORD
### SCENE 4 — INT. LAW OFFICE — DAY — 2020
An adult Daniel lays copies of records on a conference table. THE CIVIL ATTORNEY studies them.

**ATTORNEY:** If we file, they'll challenge what happened, what they knew, when they knew it, and what the law permits now.
**DANIEL:** And I get to answer?
**ATTORNEY:** That's the point of the process.
**DANIEL:** Then file it.

### SCENE 5 — INT. RECORDS ROOM — DAY — 2024

**DANIEL:** Then there has to be another record.

**DANIEL:** I know. Keep looking.
