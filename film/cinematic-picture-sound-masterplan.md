# THE CASE THEY CAN'T SILENCE — PICTURE & SOUND MASTER PLAN
**Target 90-minute dramatic feature | $0 additional spending | Production design draft, not finished footage**

## Director's intention
A compelling, cinematic dramatization about a man's account of alleged childhood abuse and his subsequent legal fight with Big Brothers Big Sisters of America and Big Brothers Big Sisters of Central Arizona. The movie should make audiences feel the character's pain, frustration, fear, resolve, and anger without inventing wrongdoing by identifiable people. Real court filings and arguments are accurately presented, including any material rebuttals. The case remains disputed.

## Picture master specification
- Widescreen 16:9 framing, target 3840×2160 UHD where source footage, rendering resources, and free software support it; acceptable working master 1920×1080 HD.
- 24 frames per second cinematic pacing, with consistent character appearance/costume and period-correct Phoenix sets.
- Color design: 1983 warm faded film stock, dusty oranges and soft natural light; 2005 muted neutrals; 2020–2026 cold natural blues, desaturated legal offices, amber home scenes.
- True scene-based performance: consistent actors in two-shots, over-the-shoulders and close-ups; accurate lip sync, real blocking, natural reactions. Avoid narration over slide decks.
- Avoid digitally impersonating real named attorneys, trustees, judges, or the alleged abuser. Source-labeled real names may appear in authentic court document graphics; fictional dialogue belongs to fictional/composite roles.
- Export clean subtitles and accessibility captions; provide original source references in credits/end-title website.

## Audio technical targets
- 48 kHz, 24-bit production audio if available; stereo exhibition master, mono dialogue center compatible.
- Dialogue clarity is highest priority. Natural breaths and ambient room tone, no robotic voice artifacts.
- Free-license sound library or self-recorded fan hum, courtroom hallway, document paper, rural Arizona cicadas, telephone vibration, car/footsteps. Verify license before distribution.
- Keep the musical emotional rise proportionate to the scene; do not drown child or survivor voices.
- Loudness and peaks for web platform delivery should be checked on export; no invented guarantee of broadcast certification.

## Performance score to match emotion
- Grief: fewer cuts, actors' faces, room tone, a soft piano note *after* the line.
- Anger: tension in the voice and body, short cuts at peaks, often no score at all.
- Frustration: repeating household noises, distant traffic, tired cadence.
- Resolve: measured wide framing, motif returns in a lower register.
- Defense/tough cross-examination: forceful but legally plausible litigation speech sourced to filed arguments; never invent threats or admissions of wrongdoing.

## Free-only production workflow
1. Expand the existing film treatment into an approximately 90-page scripted feature with act dialogue and shot coverage.
2. Build a fact/fiction ledger keyed to verified PDFs, dates, names, and specific public quotations.
3. Use free lawful original score creation (human performed or free local tools) and CC0 / verified-license foley; record voice actors with consent or use free-tier generic original voices only if use licenses permit.
4. Render individual acted scenes where free quotas allow, save each verified clip, assemble using free desktop editor (e.g. Kdenlive or DaVinci Resolve free).
5. Check lip sync/continuity and mix music; export 1080p or 4K **only if actual source footage is high-quality**. Upscaling alone does not create authentic detail.
6. Do not initiate paid rendering, credits, subscriptions, equipment purchase, or expensive tools. GitHub Pages hosts story boards, scripts, trailers and links—not computational video rendering.
