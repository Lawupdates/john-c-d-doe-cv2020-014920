## THE LETTER — INT. SMALL PHOENIX KITCHEN — NIGHT — OCTOBER 2026
Fluorescent light. Papers have colonized the table. A 63-year-old survivor, DANIEL, has been reading for hours. He picks up the defendants' filing dated October 13, 2026. The CAMERA does not turn the lawyers into pantomime villains; the pressure is in the actual procedural demands. He reads the heading of a request for sanctions. His jaw locks.

DANIEL: “I thought I was suing them.”
He lets out a short laugh, almost disbelieving.
DANIEL: “How did I become the one defending myself?”

The phone rings. DANIEL answers.

LAWYER COMPOSITE (phone, careful, tired): “They're contesting your standing to file. And they're asking the court for sanctions.”
DANIEL: “I've been carrying this since I was a child. I ask the court not to throw away my case and this is the answer?”
LAWYER: “They've made an argument. It isn't a ruling.”
DANIEL: “That distinction doesn't make it hurt any less.”

He rubs his eyes. His voice goes quiet.

DANIEL: “I wanted somebody to tell me what happened mattered.”

A long beat. The lawyer doesn't fill it with advice.

LAWYER: “I hear you.”

DANIEL: “Then don't explain me away.”

He places the telephone on the table. His hands shake. He doesn't smash anything. He takes out his October 5 motion and circles a sentence in pencil.

## THE HALLWAY — INT. COURTHOUSE — DAY
Composite DEFENSE COUNSEL and TRUSTEE COUNSEL face each other outside a hearing room. Papers in binders. People waiting silently.

DEFENSE COUNSEL (composite, low, brisk): “We believe the framework resolved the case.”
TRUSTEE COUNSEL (composite): “Then the agreement has to survive examination.”
DEFENSE COUNSEL: “And we think it will.”
The lawyer turns and notices Daniel in the distance. There is no gloating and no false intimidation. A court attendant calls for the next matter.

Daniel passes within a few feet. Not a word. The absence of a showdown feels worse than one.

## Director's emotional instructions
- PAIN: long silence and quiet domestic details; no graphic child-abuse reenactment.
- FRUSTRATION: repeated calls, pages with contradictory-sounding terms, returned emails, the waiting.
- ANGER: rapid speech only at peaks, and moments when he pulls himself back; avoid nonstop shouting.
- SADNESS: a small, human statement, a memory of family, a photograph.
- AGGRESSION: accurately portray the adversarial effect of filed motions and hearing positions; do not invent real attorneys threatening or insulting the survivor.
- COMPLEXITY: counsel are full characters with defensible perspectives rather than uniformly evil, enabling the audience to judge the documented record.
- CAMERA: natural 16:9, sustained over-the-shoulder dialogue, genuine reactions, no slideshow, no voice clone of real people.
- SOUND: mostly diegetic (fan, clock, hallway, phone); restrained original music at emotional turn.
- EVIDENCE: authenticated PDF inserts with date, exact quotation and citation; not invented court papers.
