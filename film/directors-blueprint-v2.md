# THE CASE THEY CAN'T SILENCE — DIRECTOR'S BLUEPRINT v2
**90-minute target | character-driven legal thriller | $0-budget development | 27 October 2026**

## Creative north star
Not a lecture, not a narrated podcast with moving pictures. A compelling feature about a survivor whose search for accountability across decades collides with a contested proposed resolution. The dramatic question: **Who gets to decide when his case is over?**

The pending court dispute remains unresolved in this depiction. All disputed allegations are presented as allegations, not proven crimes or professional misconduct. Maintain emotional force without misleading claims.

## Hook — 0:00–3:30 (COLD OPEN, 2026)
INT. PHOENIX HOME — 2:13 AM. A screen glows. Sound of an old ceiling fan. Adult Daniel (fictionalized portrayal of claimant) scrolls through the October 2026 defense response. A page header: Motion to Strike. He sees “sanctions.” CLOSE-UP on his face, not the word.

## Story engine
Three interwoven timelines, each visually distinctive:
- **1983–2005: Trust and memory.** Intimate domestic scenes, family dynamics, child viewpoint, no graphic abuse depiction.
- **2020–2025: Records and resolve.** A mystery driven by documents, missing information, old insurance, and exhausting preparation.
- **2026: The price of litigation.** Email threads, motions, bankruptcy estate rights, competing interpretations of mediation, pressure of deadlines.

Use transitions by objects: sealed envelope -> court notice; bike wheel -> chair caster; desk lamp in 1983 -> kitchen lamp in 2026.

## Five premium drama devices
1. **Cold opens and act-out reversals:** end an act with the document that changes the protagonist's understanding, not a fake conspiracy revelation.
2. **Multiple perspectives:** include an accurate good-faith defense argument and trustee's fiduciary duties without reducing the roles to caricatures.
3. **Performance beats:** silence, interrupted sentences, offhand humor, visible exhaustion, and small physical actions build empathy more than speeches.
4. **Evidence texture:** stamped motions, dated notices, financial statements, insurance records, clock times and court dockets; source-verified inserts, no fabricated letterheads.
5. **Dual sound palette:** 1983 ambient ballfield/crickets/tape hiss; present-day phone vibrate, keyboard, courtroom HVAC. Commission-free or self-created original music only.

## Performance and shot bible
Feature-style 24 fps, horizontal 16:9. Eye-line continuity, consistent hair/wardrobe by timeline, ambient room tone, realistic lip-sync, distinct natural voices. Use a two-shot for conflict, single close-up only at the emotional turn; keep 3–8 second natural reaction shots. Avoid entire-feature AI slideshow, generic stock-office B-roll, false archival footage, synthetic photographic impersonation, and overwrought scare music.

Alleged deception, negligence, conflicts, financial contradictions, abuse and misconduct: attribute explicitly, check corroboration and defense responses; avoid declaring judicial findings that don't exist. Fictional dialogue must be identified within film credits and by character composite in production notes; do not create false quotes attributed to real persons.
