How to Find a Sexual Abuse Lawyer

Find a civil attorney, check their license and public discipline history, and prepare for a first consultation about adult or childhood sexual abuse.

Find a lawyer Verify a lawyer Consultation questions Check court experience

Find a civil sexual abuse lawyer

  1. Start with the state connected to the case. Ask a referral service for a lawyer who handles civil sexual abuse claims, including claims against an institution if applicable.
  2. Ask the lawyer which jurisdictions they can appear in, whether they would work with local counsel, and who would be responsible for your case.
  3. Confirm the lawyer’s full name and bar number through the regulator listed in the next section. Ask for a conflict check before sharing sensitive facts or records.
  • ABA lawyer referral directory for state and local bar referral services.
  • Legal Services Corporation locator and USAGov’s legal aid directory for civil legal aid options. Eligibility, available services and whether a program can handle your type of case vary.
  • Victim Rights Law Center directory entry for an example of survivor-focused legal services in Massachusetts. Ask about service area and scope when contacting a provider.

Verify an attorney’s license and public discipline

Match the full name and bar number, especially if several lawyers have similar names. Check the current status shown by the regulator. If the status is unclear, ask the regulator whether the lawyer is authorized to practice. Where the registry links to public discipline orders, read the orders for the dates and outcome.

For any state, use the ABA directory of lawyer disciplinary agencies to locate its regulator. The following states have public lookup resources.

Questions to ask a sexual abuse attorney

What deadline and venue would you evaluate first?
Ask about the dates, jurisdiction, defendant, applicable filing rules, and any prior cases. If a public institution is involved, ask if a notice deadline needs immediate review. Let counsel assess whether a revival law could apply.
Have you handled civil cases with similar facts?
Ask about experience with individual and institutional defendants, trial and appeals, confidentiality, and related proceedings such as bankruptcy. Where a matter is public, you can request a case number and look up the attorney’s role.
Who would handle my case and how would we communicate?
Ask who would conduct interviews, communicate with the defense, and appear at hearings. Discuss a safe way for the office to contact you and whether you can bring a support person to the consultation.
How are fees and case expenses handled?
Ask for the agreement in writing. If the lawyer proposes a contingency fee, ask what the percentage applies to, when costs are deducted, who advances expenses, and whether anyone owes expenses if there is no recovery. Ask how appeals, liens and co-counsel fees are handled. The ABA model fee rule is a reference point. State rules govern the engagement.
What can remain private at each stage?
Ask how the firm handles medical records, sensitive intake materials, public filings, and requests to proceed under a pseudonym. Ask what can be disclosed at filing, discovery, trial, or settlement before deciding what to share.

For the first conversation, have a short timeline, the states or counties involved, and any existing case numbers or orders available. Ask the office for a secure way to send sensitive documents after the conflict check.

Use public dockets to check court experience

If a lawyer mentions a public case, find the filing by case number and look for their name on an appearance, pleading, or order. Read the judgment or order when checking the outcome. A lawyer’s name in a case shows a role in that proceeding. It takes the documents to understand that role.
